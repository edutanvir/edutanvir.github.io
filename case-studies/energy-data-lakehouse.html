Project overview

Making energy data easier to analyze.

Energy information comes from different sources, uses different structures, and measures different parts of the sector. This project brings selected datasets into one analytical environment and presents them in an interactive dashboard.

The challenge

Prepare energy-related data for consistent exploration, comparison, and reporting.

The engineering

Use Fabric and PySpark to organize and transform datasets for analytical use.

The outcome

Present energy generation, emissions, and pricing measures in a report built for exploration.

These are questions for exploration, not conclusions about causation. Read each measure with its source, units, date range, and data coverage in mind.

Engineering decisions

Why the data workflow matters.

Data consistency

Before energy figures can be compared, dates, geographic names, energy-source labels, and units need one consistent representation. Different source definitions can otherwise produce misleading comparisons.

Transformation and validation

Data preparation should keep transformations traceable and flag missing values, unexpected records, and incompatible data types.

Analytics-ready reporting

A well-structured analytical dataset makes report measures easier to maintain and interpret. The dashboard states what each measure represents and the period and geography it covers.

Limitations and next steps

Good analysis starts with context.

Energy datasets differ in reporting periods, geographic coverage, measurement units, and definitions. These differences should be checked before drawing conclusions from comparisons.

Document the sources

Record the dataset name, publisher, retrieval date, and relevant definitions.

Validate the measures

Confirm units, aggregation rules, missing periods, and the meaning of each KPI.

Improve reproducibility

Document the transformation workflow and add repeatable data-quality checks.
